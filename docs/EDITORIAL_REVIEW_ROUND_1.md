# Zero Sum — Editorial Review Round 1

**Date:** 2026-11-06  
**Source:** [English branching screenplay v0.1](../screenplay/ZERO_SUM_SCREENPLAY_DRAFT_EN_V0.1.md) and [recovered narrative treatment](DETAILED_NARRATIVE_TREATMENT.md).  
**Status:** Advisory editorial findings, **not owner-approved screenplay changes**. Existing accepted playable application and v0.1 screenplay are untouched.

## Verdict

**Strong plot-level fidelity; needs a targeted v0.2 dramatic revision.** Nine pathways and core philosophical constraints remain recognizable. No need to reopen branch topology, introduce additional endings or expand technology. The source is newly authored dialogue and stage direction, not recovered verbatim lines. Different outcomes are not moral grades.

## High-priority continuity and dramatic issues

1. **Team vehicles at S00/S01.** The draft shows all four operatives inside the single pursuing vehicle, then immediately permits Alex to join one of two independent teams. Original creative intent assumes the squad can split. Stage two synchronized pursuit vehicles before the fork, with a credible way for Alex to choose which vehicle/team to accompany (e.g. established transfer before junction, not magical instantaneous relocation). Do not presume an unapproved specialized transport mechanic; select the simplest staging.
2. **Distinctive catastrophe endings (B/C/E/F).** The device's sphere outcome is identical by canon; dramatic meanings are not. B: Alex's doomed interior race after refusing collateral killing; C: face-to-face glimpse of Karl's convictions; E: knowledge that Jacque lives because Alex rescued him, without Alex receiving omniscient exposition; F: exterior view of Karl after the rescue choice. Avoid repeating essentially the same final wide shot and one-sentence summary as the sole differentiator.
3. **Whose knowledge?** Some prose reads like an omniscient explanation of the alternate endings, e.g. B: “There is no moment in which Alex learns what another choice might have done” and E: “That truth neither reverses the destruction nor diminishes the rescue.” These sentences express the theme, but would be stronger as editorial notes, not viewer-facing narration. The audience may infer them.

## Character and pacing development

4. **Pete:** Appears briefly at the start, dies offscreen on route M, and becomes Misaki's partner on route J. His reliability and emotional compatibility should be demonstrated by small concrete actions in both shared opening and surviving J-route hospital radio report; no invented professional specialty or expanded romantic subplot.
5. **Alex and Misaki:** Alex's emotional investment is underplayed on the page relative to established mutual potential. Preserve a disciplined, restrained attraction conveyed through reaction rather than love-confession; no change to Misaki's explicit rational recommendation at the fork.
6. **Misaki after injury:** She can insist Alex leave and simultaneously feel abandoned. Preserve this ambivalence even in the G route where he stays. Avoid making either professional duty or relational desire an absolute moral instruction.
7. **Ending I:** Journey from effective CBD intervention and catastrophic loss to anonymous vigilante needs credible emotional progression and a passage of time; avoid a sudden stylistic jump that makes vigilante behavior a rewarded hero upgrade. Maintain emotional distance from Misaki, Jacque's loss and unanswered grief.
8. **Ending H:** The career-ending inquiry must be plausible and complex rather than an automatic unfair punishment. Show competing institutional/public interpretations without assigning the story a single moral verdict.
9. **Ending A vs D:** Both medal-at-grave scenes are canon. Differentiate their emotions and consequences: A includes the civilian deaths by overpenetration and protest; D includes successful nonfatal capture of Karl, alongside Jacque's death and the independent CBD assault's cost.

## Dialogue / visual revision opportunities

10. The Karl line “There will be people after it” in Ending C could imply an unexplained survival/new-world mechanism; his ideological continuity—not literal resurrection or guaranteed post-catastrophe society—must remain unmistakable. His rhetoric can be fanatical without explaining the theme to the viewer.
11. Several lines and stage directions state morals directly (“the choice did not become harmless”; “that truth neither reverses the destruction”; “we don't get to know”). Retain useful internal thematic notes but let images, hesitation, and reactions carry much of the onscreen meaning. Do not erase authentic conversation merely to avoid explicit lines.
12. **Action geography:** Explain Alex's access to an exterior ascent jetpack without contradicting Jacque's damaged jetpack; ensure the tower hangar/car/sniper vantage relationships work. Keep sphere edge precise, clean, white, opaque and bounded before Downtown.
13. **Relative length:** Keep uneven duration across branches. G/H/I need space for their aftermath; B/E may justifiably end swiftly, and the opening can be shared. Do not prescribe uniform scene counts or timing.

## Proposed v0.2 acceptance tests (creative)

- All nine routes map to the established decisions and endings without changed fates.
- Choices are rational from Alex's perspective; no cross-route knowledge or explicit good/bad scoring.
- Stronger Pete, Jacque, Misaki and Alex differentiation with economy of dialogue.
- Independently coherent endings, especially differing dramatic implications of B/C/E/F.
- A believable two-vehicle opening and physically consistent pursuit/jetpack staging.
- No new canonical claims added silently; newly authored dialogue remains proposed until owner reviews.
- No changes to accepted M1/M2 runtime, established art or production/deployment state.
